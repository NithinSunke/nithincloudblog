1. What is cloud computing?

Cloud computing means using IT resources (servers, storage, databases, networks) over the internet, on demand, and paying only for what you use. You don't buy hardware or run a data center. The provider does that, and you provision resources in minutes from a web console, CLI or API.

The five characteristics (NIST definition)

On-demand self-serviceProvision resources yourself, with no ticket to a vendor.
Broad network accessReach services from anywhere over the network.
Resource poolingMany customers share the provider's hardware securely.
Rapid elasticityScale up or down quickly as demand changes.
Measured serviceUsage is metered; you pay for what you consume.
CapEx vs OpExOn-premises IT is capital expenditure (buy servers upfront). Cloud is operational expenditure (pay monthly for use). This is one of the most common exam themes.

2. Service models: IaaS, PaaS, SaaS

The service model decides who manages what. Think of it as: how much of the stack does the provider run for me?

DBA analogyRunning Oracle Database on a Compute VM is IaaS (you patch and back up). Autonomous Database is PaaS (Oracle patches, tunes and backs it up).

4. OCI physical architecture

This is the most tested area of this topic. OCI is organized in three nested layers:

  • Region: a localized geographic area, such as India South (Hyderabad) or India West (Mumbai). Each region is independent. Choose regions by proximity to users, data residency laws and service availability.
  • Availability Domain (AD): one or more isolated data centers inside a region. ADs don't share power or cooling, so a failure in one shouldn't affect another.
  • Fault Domain (FD): a grouping of hardware inside an AD. Every AD has three fault domains. Spreading instances across FDs protects you from a single hardware failure or planned maintenance.
Key points for the exam
  • ADs in a region are linked by a low-latency, high-bandwidth network, so you can replicate data synchronously between them.
  • In a single-AD region, get high availability by spreading resources across the three fault domains.
  • For disaster recovery, replicate to a second region. OCI offers region pairs in many countries for this.
  • Realm: a logical collection of regions (commercial or government realms). Realms don't share data.
Protect against…Use
A single server or rack failing / planned maintenanceMultiple fault domains
A whole data center going downMultiple availability domains
An entire region going downA second region (DR)

5. Tenancy and compartments

When you sign up, OCI creates a tenancy: your secure, isolated account. The tenancy is also the root compartment.

A compartment is a logical container used to organize and isolate resources and to control access with IAM policies.

  • Every resource belongs to exactly one compartment.
  • Compartments can be nested up to six levels deep.
  • Compartments are global: they span all regions in your tenancy.
  • A resource can be moved to another compartment.
  • Policies attached to a parent compartment are inherited by its children.
DBA analogyA compartment is like a schema. It groups objects, and privileges decide who can touch them.

7. What makes OCI different

  • Off-box network virtualization: network and I/O virtualization run on separate hardware, isolating tenants and freeing host CPU.
  • Flat, non-oversubscribed network: predictable latency and bandwidth.
  • Consistent global pricing: the same price in every commercial region.
  • First-class Oracle Database support: Exadata and Autonomous Database are native services.
  • Generous Free Tier: a 30-day trial credit plus Always Free resources that never expire.

8. Pricing and the Free Tier

Two consumption options: Pay As You Go (no commitment, billed on use) and Annual Universal Credits (committed spend, usually at a discount).

Free trialUS$300 in credits for 30 days to try most services.
Always FreeNo time limit: small Compute VMs (AMD micro, Arm Ampere A1), two Autonomous Databases, Block Volume, Object Storage and a Load Balancer.
Heads upLimits change over time. Check Oracle's Free Tier page for current allowances before you build.

9. Shared responsibility model

Security is shared. Oracle secures the cloud itself: data centers, hardware, hypervisor and network. You secure what you put in the cloud: your data, user access, IAM policies, OS patching on IaaS, and network rules.

Hands-on lab: create your first compartment

Time: about 10 minutes. Cost: free (compartments themselves cost nothing).

  1. Sign up at oracle.com/cloud/free if you haven't already, and pick a home region close to you (e.g. India South – Hyderabad). The home region can't be changed later.
  2. Sign in to the OCI Console. Note your tenancy name and the region shown in the top bar.
  3. Open the navigation menu → Identity & Security → Compartments. You'll see your tenancy listed as the root (root).
  4. Click Create Compartment. Name: Sandbox; Description: Learning labs; Parent: your root compartment. Click Create.
  5. Repeat to create Dev with Sandbox as its parent. You now have a two-level hierarchy.
  6. Open any service page (e.g. Compute → Instances) and use the Compartment picker on the left to switch into Sandbox. Every lab from now on lives here.
Check yourselfSwitch the region in the top bar. Is your Sandbox compartment still there? (It should be. Compartments are global.)

Exam tips

  • Know the hierarchy cold: Region → Availability Domain → Fault Domain, with three FDs per AD.
  • Single-AD region + high availability = spread across fault domains.
  • Disaster recovery = a second region.
  • Compartments are global; most resources are regional; some (like a compute instance) are AD-specific.
  • The tenancy is the root compartment.
  • CapEx → OpEx and "pay only for what you use" appear often in cloud-benefit questions.
  • Watch for distractors that confuse availability domains with regions.

Quiz: test yourself

Click an answer to see if you're right. Your score appears at the end.

Q1. How many fault domains does every availability domain have?

Every AD contains exactly three fault domains.

Q2. A region has only one availability domain. How do you make an application highly available there?

In single-AD regions, high availability comes from placing resources in different fault domains.

Q3. What protects you against the loss of an entire region?

Disaster recovery for a regional outage means a second region, often a region pair in the same country.

Q4. Autonomous Database is an example of which service model?

Oracle patches, tunes and backs up Autonomous Database for you, which makes it PaaS.

Q5. You install Oracle Database yourself on a Compute VM. Which model is this?

You manage the OS and database on rented infrastructure: IaaS.

Q6. What is the scope of a compartment?

Compartments are global; the resources inside them are mostly regional.

Q7. What is the root compartment called?

When you sign up, the tenancy is created and acts as the root compartment.

Q8. How deep can compartments be nested?

Compartments can be nested up to six levels.

Q9. Moving from buying servers upfront to paying monthly for usage is a shift from…

Cloud turns capital expenditure into operational expenditure.

Q10. On a Compute VM, who patches the guest operating system?

Under the shared responsibility model, OS patching on IaaS is the customer's job.
